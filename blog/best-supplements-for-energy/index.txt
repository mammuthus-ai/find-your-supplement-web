In a Dutch study of 571 people who saw their family doctor about tiredness, 65 percent reported sleep problems and 61 percent reported distress or worry, while only about 8 percent were found to have a clear physical illness within a year. Anemia turned up in 9 people and low B12 in just 1 (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19858240/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Nijrolder et al., 2009</a>). Doctors are advised to look at sleep, lifestyle, mood, and medicines first (<a href="https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2023/0700/fatigue-adults.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Latimer et al., 2023</a>; <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/tiredness-and-fatigue/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NHS</a>).5:T596,If you've been tired for weeks without a clear reason, see your doctor. Common first blood tests include a full blood count (for anemia), ferritin (your iron stores), thyroid function, and blood sugar (HbA1c), with B12 added if your doctor thinks it's relevant (<a href="https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng206/chapter/Recommendations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NICE's ME/CFS guideline, NG206</a>; <a href="https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2023/0700/fatigue-adults.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Latimer et al., 2023</a>). Chronic stress can wreck sleep and leave you drained, but despite the internet, "adrenal fatigue" isn't a real diagnosis (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27557747/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Cadegiani and Kater, 2016</a>). And caffeine doesn't add energy. It hides sleepiness by blocking a brain signal called adenosine, and too much, or too late in the day, can cut into your sleep, even when taken 6 hours before bed (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24235903/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Drake et al., 2013</a>).6:T53e,It's easy to miss: the body stores years' worth of B12, so symptoms creep up slowly and look like many other things (<a href="https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2017/0915/p384.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Langan and Goodbred, 2017</a>). The standard blood test isn't perfect either. Results can land in a borderline zone, and doctors can then order a follow-up test (methylmalonic acid, or "active B12") (<a href="https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NICE NG239</a>). Reviews of studies in vegetarians and vegans have found B12 deficiency rates anywhere from near zero to over 80 percent, depending on the group and whether people took supplements, and vegans who don't supplement are at the highest risk (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23356638/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Pawlak et al., 2013</a>; <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24667752/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Pawlak et al., 2014</a>).7:T56a,<strong>Dose:</strong> to prevent deficiency (for example, if you're vegan), about 50 mcg a day, or a larger weekly dose, works for most people who absorb B12 normally (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29499976/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Del Bo' et al., 2019</a>). Older adults who are mildly deficient needed about 650 to 1,000 mcg a day to correct it in one trial (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15911731/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Eussen et al., 2005</a>), and people with absorption problems are usually prescribed at least <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=vitamin+b12+cyanocobalamin+1000+mcg&tag=fysarticles-20" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">1,000 mcg a day</a> (<a href="https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NICE NG239</a>). If a doctor confirms you're deficient, treatment is often 1,000 to 2,000 mcg a day by mouth, but some causes, like pernicious anemia, need injections. Get the cause diagnosed rather than self-treating.8:T5a9,The best positive evidence is small. A 2016 trial in 120 young adults who were truly deficient (below 20 ng/mL), and who had other causes of tiredness ruled out, found a single vitamin D dose modestly reduced tiredness after 4 weeks (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28033244/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Nowak et al., 2016</a>). Large trials in mostly healthy older adults with normal levels found 2,000 IU a day didn't improve physical performance, falls, or frailty (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37325962/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">LeBoff et al., 2023</a>). In the US, about 1 in 20 people are at risk of true deficiency and nearly 1 in 4 are below the 20 ng/mL level experts call adequate (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31076739/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Herrick et al., 2019</a>). Rates are higher in winter, in people with darker skin, in people who live far from the equator, and in people who spend most of the day indoors (<a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NIH ODS</a>).9:T5a9,Iron is central to hemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen from your lungs to the rest of your body. Run low, and you make less of it, which can cause the fatigue, weakness, and shortness of breath on exertion of iron deficiency anemia (<a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NIH ODS</a>; <a href="https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/anaemia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">WHO</a>). Iron deficiency is the most common nutritional cause of anemia worldwide (<a href="https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/anaemia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">WHO</a>). It's most likely in women with heavy periods, pregnant women, frequent blood donors, endurance athletes (particularly women), vegetarians and vegans, and people with celiac disease or IBD. Plant (non-heme) iron is absorbed much less well than the heme iron in meat, roughly 2 to 20 percent versus 15 to 35 percent (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3999603/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Abbaspour et al., 2014</a>).a:T541,<strong>Important:</strong> get a blood test first: a full blood count plus ferritin (your iron stores), and transferrin saturation if you have an inflammatory condition. Too much iron is harmful. High doses upset the stomach, very high doses can be dangerous, and the adult upper limit is 45 mg a day unless a doctor prescribes more (<a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NIH ODS</a>). If a test shows low iron, your doctor should also look for the cause, such as heavy periods, gut bleeding, or celiac disease, rather than just topping you up (<a href="https://gastro.org/clinical-guidance/gastrointestinal-evaluation-of-iron-deficiency-anemia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">AGA</a>). If you are low, a gentler form such as ferrous bisglycinate may cause fewer stomach side effects than ferrous sulfate. The evidence is strongest in pregnancy and comes from small trials (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36728680/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Fischer et al., 2023</a>). Your doctor will choose the dose.b:T562,Most ATP, the cell's energy currency, works as a complex bound to magnesium, so the many reactions that use ATP depend on it. Real magnesium deficiency can impair energy production, but it is uncommon in healthy people (<a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NIH ODS</a>). About half of Americans get less magnesium from food than they need on average (48 percent in national survey data), though low intake isn't the same as deficiency (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22364157/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Rosanoff et al., 2012</a>). Early signs of deficiency are vague (tiredness, weakness, poor appetite, nausea), and cramps and twitching come later. These symptoms have many other causes. A normal blood magnesium test doesn't rule out low body stores, and magnesium isn't part of a routine tiredness workup. Small studies in older adults with insomnia suggest magnesium may help sleep a little, but the evidence is weak (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33865376/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Mah and Pitre, 2021</a>).c:T454,<strong>Dose:</strong> typical supplement doses are 200 to 350 mg of elemental magnesium a day. Don't go above 350 mg from supplements unless a doctor advises it, because higher doses commonly cause diarrhea and cramping (<a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NIH ODS</a>). Avoid magnesium supplements if you have kidney disease unless your doctor says otherwise. Forms that dissolve well, such as <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=magnesium+citrate+200+mg&tag=fysarticles-20" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">magnesium citrate</a>, tend to be absorbed better than magnesium oxide. There's no good evidence that any one form, including malate, boosts energy (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8587088/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Russell et al., 1995</a>).d:T496,CoQ10 sits in the inner membrane of mitochondria and plays an essential role in the electron transport chain, which makes most of the body's ATP. CoQ10 levels in several organs appear to fall after early adulthood, based on small autopsy studies, but whether this causes tiredness, or whether supplements reverse it, is not established (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2779364/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Kalén et al., 1989</a>). Statins lower blood CoQ10 levels because they block a pathway that makes both cholesterol and CoQ10 (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26192349/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Banach et al., 2015</a>). Individual trials vary, but overall the evidence doesn't show CoQ10 relieves statin-related muscle aches (<a href="https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NCCIH</a>). Don't stop or change a statin without your doctor.e:T472,<strong>Dose:</strong> trials in that review used about 60 to 500 mg a day, most often 100 to 300 mg, taken with a meal that contains fat (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36091835/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Tsai et al., 2022</a>). CoQ10 comes as ubiquinone or ubiquinol, and the body converts between them. An oil-based softgel seems to matter more for absorption than the form (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30153575/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">López-Lluch et al., 2019</a>). Ubiquinol costs more, and the evidence that it works better, at any age, is limited. <strong>Safety:</strong> CoQ10 may interact with warfarin (a blood thinner) and insulin. Check with your doctor or pharmacist first if you take these or are having cancer treatment (<a href="https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NCCIH</a>).f:T80f,Rather than fixing a deficiency, ashwagandha is studied for the stress response. Several small trials, mostly in stressed adults in India and often using product supplied by the extract maker, found the KSM-66 extract lowered self-reported stress (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23439798/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Chandrasekhar et al., 2012</a>; <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32021735/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Salve et al., 2019</a>). In those trials, morning cortisol fell roughly 15 to 30 percent versus placebo at 250 to 600 mg a day, but cortisol is a lab marker, and lower cortisol doesn't automatically mean more energy. Evidence for fatigue specifically is thinner. A 2021 trial in 50 athletic adults found 600 mg a day for 8 weeks modestly improved VO2max and self-rated recovery and energy versus placebo. It was a single small study, and the results may not apply to non-athletes (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33600918/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Tiwari et al., 2021</a>). One small trial in men starting weight training found smaller rises in a muscle-damage marker (creatine kinase) (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26609282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Wankhede et al., 2015</a>). Some people may feel less stressed on <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=ashwagandha+ksm-66+300+mg&tag=fysarticles-20" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">ashwagandha</a> (<a href="https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NCCIH</a>).10:T46c,<strong>Safety:</strong> not for use if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, or have thyroid or autoimmune disease or liver problems. Rare cases of liver injury have been reported (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31643854/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">LiverTox</a>). Stop and see a doctor if you notice yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, or itching. It may interact with thyroid, diabetes, blood-pressure, sedative, and immune-suppressing drugs (<a href="https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NCCIH</a>). Denmark's food safety agency has banned its sale in food and supplements, citing possible effects on hormones (<a href="https://foedevarestyrelsen.dk/kost-og-foedevarer/alt-om-mad/kemi-i-maden/mad-med-uoensket-kemi/ashwagandha" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Danish Veterinary and Food Administration</a>, in Danish).11:T6be,<strong>High-dose B vitamins:</strong> if you're not deficient, there's little good evidence that they boost energy. Megadoses aren't always harmless either: long-term high-dose B6 can damage nerves (<a href="https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-b/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NHS</a>). <strong>Ginseng</strong> may give a small reduction in fatigue, but the studies are low quality and results conflict. Its most common side effect is insomnia, and it may affect blood clotting and blood sugar, so check with your doctor if you take blood thinners or diabetes medicine (<a href="https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NCCIH</a>). Ginseng products also vary widely in their active compounds, with up to 36-fold differences in one analysis, so a store product may not match what was studied (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11382666/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">Harkey et al., 2001</a>). <strong>Proprietary energy blends</strong> only have to list the blend's total weight, so you often can't tell how much caffeine or herbal stimulant is in each serving (<a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-C/section-101.36" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">21 CFR 101.36</a>).
