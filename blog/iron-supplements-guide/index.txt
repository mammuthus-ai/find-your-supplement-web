3:T458,Ferrous bisglycinate is iron bound to two glycine amino acids — chelated — and that wrapper lets it absorb through a different, gentler pathway, so less free iron is dumped into your gut. A <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10331582/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-teal-deep font-semibold hover:underline">2023 systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs</a> found it reliably raises hemoglobin and ferritin, and a <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8839493/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-teal-deep font-semibold hover:underline">randomized controlled trial in pregnant women</a> found it corrected iron deficiency with <strong>better GI tolerability</strong> than conventional salts at a lower elemental dose. A broader <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11554602/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-teal-deep font-semibold hover:underline">2024 comparison of oral iron salts</a> reached the same practical conclusion: chelated iron matches sulfate on efficacy while causing fewer complaints.4:T405,This is the part most people have never heard. A second dose taken the same day, or even the next morning, is largely wasted: it barely absorbs but still irritates your gut. Take iron <strong>every other day</strong> and hepcidin falls back to baseline, so the next dose absorbs more efficiently. A <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2589537023004637" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-teal-deep font-semibold hover:underline">2023 randomized trial</a> confirmed alternate-day dosing matches daily dosing for raising iron stores <strong>with fewer side effects</strong>.
