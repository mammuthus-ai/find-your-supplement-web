Multis usually include vitamin K1. If you take warfarin or another vitamin K antagonist, do not start or change vitamin K (K1 or K2) without your prescriber. B12 form is a non-issue too: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25820384/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">no good evidence</a> shows "active" methylcobalamin beats the cyanocobalamin in most multis.5:T43d,Several minerals can get in each other's way. Most multis contain far less calcium than that. Long-term zinc above about 50 mg/day can <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2407097/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">cause copper deficiency</a> — far more than a multi contains. High-dose iron (25 mg or more) taken with zinc can reduce zinc absorption (<a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">NIH ODS</a>). If you take separate iron, calcium or zinc supplements, spacing them out across the day is a simple fix.6:T594,Multis can also deliver nutrients some people don't need. Men and post-menopausal women rarely need extra iron; many men's and 50+ multis are iron-free. Iron overdose is <a href="https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002659.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">especially dangerous for young children</a>, and adult or prenatal multis with iron are a common source, so keep them locked away. Most multis include preformed vitamin A (retinyl palmitate), and a <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7477116/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">classic NEJM study</a> linked more than 10,000 IU a day of preformed vitamin A from supplements in early pregnancy to a nearly 5× higher rate of cranial-neural-crest birth defects. Choose a multi with no more than 10,000 IU (3,000 mcg RAE) preformed vitamin A, and pregnant women should use a prenatal. Beta-carotene doesn't carry the birth-defect risk, but smokers should avoid beta-carotene supplements (higher lung-cancer risk) (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35727271/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="font-semibold text-ink underline decoration-ember underline-offset-2">USPSTF, 2022</a>).
